\section{Candidate Metrics (Shortlist)}
\label{sec:candidate_metrics}

This thesis compares a minimal set of candidate indicator families that are (i) computable from versioned artifacts and Git history,
(ii) interpretable and comparable across heterogeneous projects, and (iii) compatible with hash-anchored evidence and compact
on-chain storage. Source-code measurement and open-source component-selection research motivate treating repository metrics as
decision inputs rather than complete quality judgments~\cite{timoteo_software_metrics_survey,li2022ossselection}. Reputation-system
research further motivates comparing multiple indicators under explicit threat scenarios~\cite{koutrouli2015reputation,schlosser2004reputation}.
Research on metric-data infrastructures additionally treats collection, storage, and exchange as engineering concerns, while
third-party assessment research shows that assessment objectives depend on the intended recipient and decision
context~\cite{khomyakov2019metricssharing,remencius2016thirdparty}.
Finally, smart-contract studies establish platform-specific measurement constraints and caution against treating one complexity
metric as a direct proxy for software quality or vulnerability~\cite{smartmetrics2023,scvulncomplexity}. The comparative study
evaluates all candidates; the PoC metric is selected only after evaluation.
